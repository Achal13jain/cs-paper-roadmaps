# Editorial policy

Papers in Order is a set of opinionated learning paths, not an exhaustive bibliography or a live feed of new publications. The goal is to help a reader build the right mental model in the right order.

## What belongs

A paper should satisfy at least one of these tests:

- It introduced a concept, architecture, system, or result that later work depends on.
- It is the clearest primary source for an important idea.
- It changed how a field is practiced or evaluated.
- It closes a genuine gap in an existing reading path.

Influence alone is not enough. A highly cited paper may be omitted when another source teaches the same idea more clearly or fits the sequence better.

## How papers are ordered

Order follows conceptual dependency before publication date. A later tutorial-quality paper may appear before an older, more difficult source when that produces a clearer learning path.

Every entry should explain:

- What the paper contributes.
- Why it belongs in this roadmap.
- What knowledge should come first.
- The one idea a reader should retain.

The Minimum Viable Reading Path (MVRP) is the smallest coherent subset that preserves the field's main conceptual arc. It is not a ranking of the "best" papers.

## Canonical versus recent work

The core paths favor durable papers whose importance can be judged with some confidence. Recent work may be included when it materially changes the mental model, but the project does not claim that every roadmap covers the latest frontier.

Roadmap timelines describe the included material, not the date the roadmap was last reviewed.

## Evidence and language

Descriptions should be specific and falsifiable. Avoid unsupported superlatives such as "every," "the first," "the default," or "state of the art" unless the linked paper or a reliable source supports the claim.

Links should point to an open primary source whenever possible: arXiv, an author page, a conference publication, or an official technical report.

## Reviews and corrections

- Automated checks validate structure and link health.
- Human review is required for importance, wording, and placement.
- Corrections are welcome through the correction issue template.
- Substantial roadmap changes should explain what moved and why in the pull request.
- Review status and meaningful content changes belong in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

## Conflicts and attribution

Contributors should disclose close involvement with a proposed paper. Self-authored work is allowed, but it receives the same relevance and sequencing review as every other submission.

Original summaries are licensed under CC BY 4.0. Paper titles, bibliographic facts, and links remain attributed to their respective authors and publishers.
